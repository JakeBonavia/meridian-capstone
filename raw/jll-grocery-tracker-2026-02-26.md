# Grocery Tracker 2026: Who's Winning the Aisle Wars

## The barbell shopper has arrived

Since early 2020, food-at-home costs have increased over 30%. While headline inflation has moderated to around 2.7%, the cumulative impact on household finances persists significantly. Nearly 80% of shoppers continue reporting supermarket price increases, exceeding all other retail categories.

This sustained price pressure has bifurcated American consumers into two groups. Affluent households experience wealth growth, fueling premium and wellness product purchases. Middle- and lower-income families face financial strain, with depleted savings and elevated credit card debt forcing value prioritization.

The outcome: "a market where the middle is getting squeezed, and growth is concentrating at both extremes."

## Private label is no longer a backup plan

Store brands have surged 30% since 2021, now representing over 21% of grocery spending — a record $282.8 billion. Nearly half of surveyed shoppers now actively select store brands over name brands to reduce expenses.

Beyond product switching, shopping behavior is fundamentally changing. Data indicates a clear trend toward frequent, abbreviated trips. Rather than weekly cart-filling resulting in checkout sticker shock, consumers make smaller, targeted excursions, often visiting multiple stores for deal selection.

Major operators like Kroger have publicly acknowledged this pattern, observing that middle-income households specifically employ "bite-sized trips" as budget management strategies.

## The great grocery divergence is underway

This consumer behavior split mirrors a parallel industry divide. Traditional supermarkets retain overwhelming store visit dominance, yet their share continues eroding.

Since 2019, traditional grocers experienced foot traffic share decline from 75.1% to 73.2%. While seemingly modest, this represents billions in lost sales across the large industry. Winners include two distinct categories: value-focused operators and fresh-format specialty grocers.

Value players like Aldi and Grocery Outlet capture dollar-stretching consumers. Aldi opened 180 new stores in 2025 and plans 180 more in 2026 through aggressive multi-year expansion. Fresh-format chains including Trader Joe's, Whole Foods, and Sprouts attract shoppers prioritizing health, wellness, and shopping experience.

Foot traffic data illustrates the narrative clearly. Kroger's same-store visit growth remained essentially flat at 0.3%, while Trader Joe's achieved 10.4% growth, Whole Foods expanded 9.8%, and Aldi increased 8.3%.

## Canada confirms the broader trend

Canada's market exhibits strikingly similar dynamics. Major Canadian grocers continue new store openings with discount formats driving substantial growth. Loblaw concentrated on No Frills operations in Ontario and Alberta and Maxi expansion in Québec. Empire expanded FreshCo in Western Canada, while Metro focused on Super C in Québec and Food Basics in Ontario. These actions directly respond to persistent consumer price sensitivity, as store food inflation exceeds the official Consumer Price Index.

This parallel trend north of the border confirms the barbell consumer dynamic represents a broader North American phenomenon rather than U.S.-specific behavior.

## Real estate fundamentals remain strong, but nuance matters

Positive news for property owners: grocery-anchored centers substantially outperform alternatives. Vacancy for grocery-anchored properties stands at 4.0%, compared to 6.3% for non-anchored centers. This advantage generates direct pricing leverage, with grocery-anchored centers commanding 4.4% NNN rent premiums.

Sector resilience through economic shocks has proven remarkable. During COVID, net absorption turned negative for four consecutive quarters and new deliveries plummeted 68%, yet rents still grew 1.5%. During the Great Recession, availability surged from 6.2% to 8.2%, but pandemic availability barely shifted, increasing less than 1 percentage point before tightening again.

Today's challenge differs. Availability compressed to 4.7%, and leasing velocity slowed as quality space becomes scarce. Recent negative 2025 net absorption reflects broader retail closures across all property types rather than grocery-specific weakness.

## Geography matters more than ever

Grocery expansion concentrates in specific markets rather than occurring uniformly. The Southeast dominates, capturing 215 of 400 tracked new store openings in 2025. Florida leads, driven by population migration, business-friendly policies, and robust consumer spending.

Significant clusters also emerge in the Mid-Atlantic, particularly New Jersey, Maryland, and the Washington D.C./Baltimore corridor. These represent different opportunities: high-density, affluent markets where operators fill gaps and upgrade existing locations rather than pursue pure greenfield development.

## The physical store is evolving, not disappearing

Online grocery sales continue steep upward trajectory, though the narrative involves stores becoming e-commerce fulfillment engines rather than store versus e-commerce competition. Total online grocery sales project to reach $228 billion by 2030, representing over 23% of total grocery spending.

Critically, e-commerce consumer preferences shifted. Home delivery surged to 45% of online grocery orders, surpassing both pickup and shipping. This reversal carries profound site selection and design implications. Properties must accommodate delivery driver staging areas, efficient multi-vehicle traffic flow, and flexible layouts handling simultaneous customer shopping and order fulfillment.

## Investors love grocery stores, but asset acquisition is getting competitive

Investor appetite for grocery-anchored assets remains robust. Transaction volume surged 42% in 2025 to nearly $11 billion. Significantly, institutional investors increased acquisition share to 27%, reaching the highest level in over a decade.

This capital influx compressed cap rates, which declined approximately 40 basis points from their 2023 cyclical peak and averaged 6.7% nationally at 2025 close.

## The bottom line

Grocery-anchored real estate's fundamental appeal hasn't disappeared; it has evolved. These assets offer superior occupancy, steady foot traffic, and operational resilience compared to other retail formats. However, the assumption that any grocery anchor delivers these benefits is now obsolete.

The future belongs to properties anchored by grocers capturing visit growth: value players serving budget-conscious households and fresh-format operators serving wellness-focused consumers. For investors, developers, and landlords, the strategic imperative is unambiguous: evaluate assets and pipeline not merely on whether they feature a grocery anchor, but whether they have the appropriate one for today's bifurcated market.
