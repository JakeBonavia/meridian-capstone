# Industry Context: Grocery Sector Trends

National grocery sector context — a "barbell" consumer split between value and premium, specialty/value formats gaining share, major retailers expanding and remodeling, and record investment in grocery-anchored real estate.

_Last updated: 2026-09-21_

## Market snapshot

- U.S. grocery sector valued at $912.4 billion; grocers reinventing formats, identities, and footprints as shopping habits evolve.
- Grocery-anchored shopping centers as a category maintain below-market vacancy and rent premiums, but performance diverges sharply by operator/format — "the days when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading" (JLL's Naveen Jaggi).

## Consumer bifurcation ("the barbell shopper")

- Food-at-home costs are up over 30% since early 2020; ~80% of shoppers say they've noticed grocery price increases more than in any other retail category.
- Shoppers have split into two groups: affluent households increasing premium/wellness spend, and middle/lower-income households under financial strain and prioritizing value — "a market where the middle is getting squeezed, and growth is concentrating at both extremes."
- Private label has grown 30% since 2021 and is now >21% of grocery spending (a record $282.8B); nearly half of shoppers actively choose store brands to save money.
- Shopping behavior is shifting toward frequent, shorter "bite-sized trips" instead of weekly cart-filling, often spreading purchases across multiple stores — a pattern Kroger has specifically attributed to middle-income households managing budgets.

## Who's winning and losing

- **Specialty/fresh-format grocers gaining share**: by revenue, Trader Joe's grew 3% YoY (period ending Feb. 28, 2026), 6 points above the sector average, with gains across income levels and age groups, including double-digit YoY growth among Gen Z (ICSC/Consumer Edge). By same-store *visit* growth, Trader Joe's is up 10.4%, Whole Foods 9.8%, and Aldi 8.3%, while Kroger is essentially flat at 0.3% (JLL Grocery Tracker 2026) — a different metric (traffic vs. revenue) but pointing the same direction.
- **Traditional supermarkets under pressure**: Publix and Safeway saw pullbacks from all income groups, sharpest among lower-income shoppers (ICSC). Traditional grocers' overall foot-traffic share slipped from 75.1% (2019) to 73.2% (JLL) — a modest-looking shift that represents billions in lost sales industry-wide.
- **Discount/value grocers**: mixed signal across sources — ICSC reports Aldi and Food 4 Less growth "plateaued since mid-2025," while JLL reports Aldi's same-store visits still growing 8.3% and store count expanding aggressively. Not necessarily contradictory (visit growth can continue while YoY comp growth flattens), but worth treating the "plateaued" framing with caution.
- Consumer Edge's read: shoppers are making "more deliberate choices," gravitating to retailers with a clear loyalty reason — value or curated experience. Undifferentiated traditional grocers face the most sustained pressure.

**Noted discrepancy — Aldi store-opening counts**: ICSC (2026-04-24) reports Aldi "opening 150 U.S. stores in 2026 for its 50th anniversary." JLL (2026-02-26) reports Aldi "opened 180 new stores in 2025 and plans 180 more in 2026." These don't reconcile (150 vs. 180 for the same 2026 figure) — flagging rather than resolving; if this matters for analysis, verify against Aldi's own reporting.

## Big retailers' physical expansion

- **Walmart**: remodeling 650+ stores in 2026 ($5.5B allocated in fiscal 2026 for 675 remodels), opening ~20 stores 2026-2027; 5,200+ U.S. stores as of Jan. 31, 2026.
- **Costco**: targeting 30+ new stores/year; ~28 net new openings expected fiscal 2026; ~640 U.S. stores.
- **Aldi**: testing a modular store format (Aventura, FL, since Sept. 2025) built for flexible sizing/urban footprints; 2026 store-count plans conflict between sources — see discrepancy note above.
- **Target**: repositioning as a specialty grocer — food & beverage did $24B+ in net sales FY2025; CMO framing it as "a truly distinctive grocery destination," not "just another grocer."

## Real estate fundamentals

- Grocery-anchored properties: 4.0% vacancy vs. 6.3% for non-anchored centers; grocery-anchored centers command a 4.4% NNN rent premium.
- Sector proved resilient through shocks: during COVID, net absorption was negative for four straight quarters and new deliveries fell 68%, yet rents still grew 1.5%. Availability barely moved during COVID (unlike the Great Recession's jump from 6.2% to 8.2%).
- Current availability has compressed to 4.7%; leasing velocity has slowed as quality space gets scarce. Recent negative 2025 net absorption reflects broad retail closures across all property types, not grocery-specific weakness.

## Geographic concentration

- New store openings are concentrated, not uniform: the Southeast captured 215 of 400 tracked U.S. openings in 2025, led by Florida (population migration, business-friendly policy, consumer spending).
- A secondary cluster is emerging in the Mid-Atlantic (New Jersey, Maryland, DC/Baltimore corridor) — high-density, affluent markets where operators fill gaps and upgrade existing locations rather than pursue greenfield development.
- No direct data on Southern California (Meridian's market) in either source ingested so far.

## E-commerce

- Online grocery sales are projected to reach $228B by 2030 (>23% of total grocery spending).
- Home delivery now accounts for 45% of online grocery orders, surpassing pickup and shipping — stores are increasingly functioning as fulfillment engines, with site-selection implications (driver staging areas, flexible layouts for simultaneous in-store shopping and order fulfillment).

## Investment activity

- U.S. grocery-anchored center deal volume: ~$11B in 2025, up 42% from 2024 (JLL Grocery Tracker 2026; also cited by ICSC).
- Institutional investors captured 27% of 2025 acquisitions — the highest share in over a decade — which has compressed cap rates to ~6.7% nationally, down ~40 bps from the 2023 peak.
- Selected 2026 deals: Brixton Capital ($29.4M Las Vegas, $100M LA); Medipower ($115M, 7 properties); Nuveen ($298M, 7 properties); Blackstone (3 portfolios, 130 properties, $6.7B across 2025-2026); Regency Centers developing a 71,400-sq-ft Whole Foods-anchored center in Redlands, CA.

## Adjacent competition

- Tillster's 2026 Phygital Index (2,144 diners surveyed): 29% visiting fast food less, 37% visiting fast-casual less, while 36% visit grocery stores more and 33% visit convenience stores more — grocery/convenience are winning dining occasions on perceived value.
- Small-format innovation: Gelson's (27 SoCal stores) opened "ReCharge by Gelson's" micromarkets (3,000-5,400 sq ft) paired with EV fast-charging hubs.

## Canadian parallel (context only, not Meridian's market)

- Canadian grocers show the same bifurcation: Loblaw (No Frills, Maxi), Empire (FreshCo), and Metro (Super C, Food Basics) are all expanding discount formats in response to food-price pressure exceeding the general CPI — suggesting the barbell pattern is a broader North American phenomenon, not U.S.-specific.

## Why this matters for Meridian

Meridian's stated positioning — prepared foods, local sourcing, smaller footprint than national chains — sits squarely in the "fresh-format/curated experience" category both sources say is gaining share nationally, the same category Trader Joe's and Whole Foods are winning in and Target is now trying to move toward. Two additional angles worth probing with Dana: (1) private label is now >21% of national grocery spend and growing — unclear whether Meridian has any store-brand strategy today; (2) the "bite-sized trips" behavior favors stores that are convenient and trustworthy for frequent, smaller visits, which may reinforce or complicate Meridian's smaller-footprint model depending on execution. Meridian is also competing for real estate and customer attention against well-capitalized players (Walmart, Costco, Aldi) expanding aggressively in the same window it's evaluating its next site — though neither source ingested so far has direct data on Southern California specifically.
